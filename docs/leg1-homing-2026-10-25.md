## Implementation

The new `leg1_pair` worker owns only motors 12 and 13 on can1. It seeks three
independent hip contacts, then three knee contacts with the hip held. During hip
homing the knee has zero position stiffness and native velocity damping, allowing
linkage motion without imposing a fixed knee encoder position. Yaw is excluded.

Active joint gains are kp=1.75 and kd=2; virtual reference speed is 0.05 rad/s.
The supporting hip during knee homing retains the previously commissioned
kp=150/kd=3 and 0.01 rad drift guard. Native drive damping is used; the rejected
host damping experiments are not retained in the implementation.

Both motors' volatile runtime torque limits (0x700B) must be reduced to 2.625 Nm
and read back successfully before enable. A disabled-motor test verified setting,
reading and restoring this register on both physical drives (originally 12 Nm).
This proves configuration access, not independent force calibration or saturation
behavior. The measured-effort abort remains 3.5 Nm. Contact effort must reach
2.3 Nm, remain stable within 0.003 rad for 0.6 s, and repeat over three independent
5-degree backoffs with <=0.01 rad spread. Loaded contacts must lie within 0.03 rad
of the known stop reference. Hip reference is the hand-confirmed 0.802815 rad;
knee uses the earlier powered loaded reference 0.739490 rad, not the relaxed
hand-set 0.875296 rad. Actual position envelopes, fresh telemetry/fault status,
45 C temperature, 0.5 rad/s encoder-derived speed and timeouts are enforced.

Both motors must acknowledge disable before stage success. Original runtime
limits are restored only with fresh disabled feedback and matching readback.
The combined audit record must be saved before session readiness is set.
`RobotState.legs` publishes scope `pitch_knee`; yaw and global linkage calibration
are excluded. Restart, rehoming, cancellation, lost feedback or faults invalidate
readiness. Arming and execution check session readiness for every selected motor.

## Normal homing separated from commissioning

The operator requested normal homing in under a minute and identified excessive
startup/verification overhead. The dominant delay was the 0.05 rad/s virtual
reference ramp plus three contact trials per motor. Those repeated trials are
now opt-in commissioning (`homing.verify_repeatability=true`). Normal `leg1_pair`
homing seeks directly from the current pose, takes one stable verified contact
per joint, and uses the legacy 0.2 rad/s reference rate. The 2.625 Nm drive cap,
contact effort/stability criteria, stop window, sensor/fault checks, speed and
thermal guards are unchanged. No fixed startup delay was introduced or retained.

Normal homing has a shared 60-second motion deadline, including both joints;
commissioning defaults to 600 seconds. A deadline failure triggers stop rather
than claiming completion. Failed stop acknowledgements can outlast that deadline.
Normal records mark one verified contact and do not claim independent
repeatability. Session readiness accepts either normal verified contact records
or successful three-contact commissioning records, after saved results and
confirmed disable. Terminal messages now identify start and completion/failure;
READY remains visible in status_reason after successful completion.

The offline ideal-following simulation for the reported hip start 2.988670 rad
and knee start 0.976768 rad completed in 26.56 seconds combined. This is a timing
regression check, not a measured hardware completion time. No powered test was
initiated for the faster normal routine. Restart the controller to load it.


## Hip/knee interface generalized to all six legs

The worker now derives each hip/knee pair and CAN bus from the canonical mapping.
`homing-hip-knee.yaml` configures all 12 pitch/knee motor IDs and excludes every
yaw motor, including disconnected 51. Per-leg stop references and directions are
independent parameters. Only leg 1's current references are populated; legs 2–6
remain explicitly unconfigured rather than inheriting leg 1 values or archived
session offsets.

`/motors/leg_N/home` selects an individual configured leg; `/motors/home` runs
`homing.legs` sequentially, with the normal 60-second budget applied per leg.
The batch validates references and transport selection before starting, stops
at its first failed leg, and preserves readiness for completed legs. Local
motor/homing faults and rehoming clear only the affected pair; global transport
or normal motion faults clear all readiness. Both home directions are supported.
The existing leg1_pair method and LegPairHoming import remain aliases for
compatibility. No separate per-leg worker modules were added.

Offline lifecycle tests exercise all six pairs with fake drives, native control
routing, enable/stop acknowledgements, torque-limit restoration, persistence,
readiness accumulation, cancellation, partial failure and real ROS service
registration/dispatch without CAN devices. No powered all-leg test was initiated.
